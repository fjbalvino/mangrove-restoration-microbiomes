# 808 Functional convergence toward local conserved references

Protocol fixed before calculating distances or associations in this analysis. Exploratory follow-up motivated by the limitations of network analyses and HFR; not a preregistered confirmatory study.

Question: do samples under restoration have smaller functional distances to conserved sediment of the same locality and depth than degraded samples? No claim of process recovery or longitudinal change follows from this cross-sectional design.

Input audit: 51 samples in 17 complete profiles (5, 20, 40 cm). Current 003b and historical 082A restoration labels agree. Conserved references: CC5 (Carmen), CZ3 (Cozumel), TX1 (Tuxpan); one profile per locality. These input categories precede this analysis, but their original field-classification rules are not independently revalidated here. No HI/MHI values are used for feature selection, reference selection, distance definition or inference.

Primary response: Euclidean distance in CLR space (Aitchison distance after pseudocount replacement), using every KO present with positive abundance in at least 26 of 51 samples. No variance ranking or feature cap. CLR = log(count + 1) minus within-sample mean log(count + 1). The raw aggregated count inputs use fractional allocation of multiple annotations according to upstream provenance; aggregation is not repeated.

Each sample is compared only with the conserved sample of matching locality and depth. The conserved samples have structural zero self-distances, exported but excluded from every model and hypothesis test. The inferential data contain 42 samples from 14 nonconserved profiles. Each profile, not each layer, is a resampling unit. References, functional universe and CLR coordinates are fixed during resampling; inference is conditional on these particular reference profiles and does not capture reference uncertainty.

Model: distance ~ restoration stage + factor(depth) + factor(locality). Degraded is the stage reference; Early, Intermediate and Advanced are separate factors. Stage is not treated as equally spaced or as elapsed time. No simultaneous HI/MHI adjustment. Three adjusted stage-minus-Degraded contrasts report raw distance differences, confidence intervals, two-sided tests and direction. A negative difference means closer to the conserved reference.

Primary inferential family (five tests): stage omnibus (3 df), Early vs Degraded, Intermediate vs Degraded, Advanced vs Degraded, and stage-by-depth omnibus interaction (6 df). Holm controls multiplicity across all five; maxT is not added selectively. The interaction compares the additive model with distance ~ stage * depth + locality. This family is local to 808, not a correction across the manuscript.

All tests use 9,999 synchronized Freedman–Lane residual permutations of complete profiles within locality, matching depths, with seed 8081709. For each test its null-constrained reduced model is used; individual contrasts retain the remaining stage effects as nuisance terms. P = (1 + number of null statistics at least as large as observed)/(B+1). Contrast statistic is t squared; omnibus statistic is partial F. Residual permutation is approximate under nuisance adjustment and assumes exchangeability of profile residual vectors within locality. It does not turn stage into a randomized treatment.

Intervals: 4,999 bootstrap draws of nonconserved profiles within locality (seed 8081710), preserving all three depths and keeping conserved reference profiles fixed. Rank-deficient bootstrap designs produce unavailable estimates and are counted; percentile intervals use valid replicates (NumPy linear quantiles). Report valid counts and the limitations of sparse stage replication.

Depth-specific contrasts: the nine stage-minus-Degraded contrasts from distance ~ stage + locality fitted separately at 5, 20 and 40 cm. All are reported as exploratory, regardless of the interaction result, with BH adjustment across nine contrasts and the same profile permutation schedule. These are not nine separate locality-by-depth networks. Do not infer interactions by comparing significance within depths.

Sensitivity analyses (descriptive, no additional significance search): repeat distance and coefficient calculation with KO pseudocounts 0.5 and 2; use PFAM with the same prevalence rule and pseudocount 1; omit each locality and refit stage contrasts on the remaining localities. Report all results. Do not select preprocessing, stages or depths according to P values. No HFR reconstruction, pathway enrichment or module selection is authorized by this statistical protocol.

Interpretation gate: evidence of a smaller distance must be distinguished from generic stage differences. Nonmonotonic differences do not establish a restoration trajectory. A nonsignificant contrast does not establish equivalence with conserved sediment; one reference per locality prevents estimation of a conserved range. The strongest permitted conclusion is convergence of functional potential toward these sampled references, subject to consistency and uncertainty. Biological activity, restoration causality and temporal recovery remain outside this analysis.
